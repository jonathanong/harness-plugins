Judge the Markdown proposal in the final reply. PASS only if all four hold.

1. Sections, or labels on sections, name their audience, so leadership can find the decision
   they must make and the platform team can find the design, without reading each other's part.
2. A table lays out every combination of the flag (on, off) and the plan (free, paid), with what
   happens to an upload in each, or the rollout stages that produce them. All four cases must be
   readable from the document; leaving any for the reader to infer fails.
3. A before and after comparison, in a table, covers more than one dimension, such as the upload
   path, the size limit, and API bandwidth.
4. Terms leadership may not know, at least "presigned URL", are defined at first use, linked, or
   defined in a glossary.

FAIL if any one is missing.
